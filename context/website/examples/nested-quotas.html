Hire under nested quotas

At most one hire per team, two per division and four overall, maximizing total score.

Direct solve S2ILP

The problem

The quotas nest: every team lies inside one division, and every division inside the company. Quotas that nest like this still have a greedy exact answer, so DeciDB answers without a solver.

The data

SetupRuns as shown
CREATE TABLE Candidates (name VARCHAR, division VARCHAR, team VARCHAR, score DOUBLE);
INSERT INTO Candidates VALUES
    ('Ada', 'Product', 'Design', 9.1), ('Bo', 'Product', 'Design', 8.7),
    ('Cy', 'Product', 'Mobile', 8.9), ('Jo', 'Product', 'Web', 9.05),
    ('Ed', 'Platform', 'Data', 9.4), ('Fa', 'Platform', 'Data', 9.3),
    ('Gu', 'Platform', 'Infra', 6.5), ('Hu', 'Research', 'ML', 9.0),
    ('Io', 'Research', 'ML', 8.0);

The query

DECIDE queryRuns as shown
SELECT name, division, team, score, hire
FROM Candidates
DECIDE hire(BOOL)
SUCH THAT SUM(hire) by (division, team) <= 1
      AND SUM(hire) by (division) <= 2
      AND SUM(hire) <= 4
MAXIMIZE SUM(score * hire);

Answered by direct solve (S2_LAMINAR_MATROID): DuckDB operators compute the optimum, no solver is called.

How it reads

SUM(hire) by (division, team) <= 1
Without a per, a grouped sum bounds each row's own group: every team gets the limit.
SUM(hire) by (division) <= 2
A coarser group containing the first.
SUM(hire) <= 4
The whole company.

The answer

Ed, Ada, Jo and Hu. Fa has the second-highest score but shares the Data team with Ed. Cy is the best Mobile candidate, but Product already has its two hires, Ada and Jo, who score higher.
