A cheap diet that meets nutrition targets

Pick servings of each food so protein, calories and fat land within their targets at the lowest cost.

INT decisionBETWEENseveral targets

The problem

The classic diet problem: at most three servings of any food, at least 90 g of protein, between 1,800 and 2,200 calories, and no more than 50 g of fat.

The data

Setup
CREATE TABLE Foods (food VARCHAR, cost DOUBLE, protein INT, calories INT, fat INT);
INSERT INTO Foods VALUES
    ('Oatmeal', 0.60, 5, 150, 3), ('Chicken', 2.40, 31, 240, 6),
    ('Eggs', 0.90, 12, 155, 10), ('Rice', 0.40, 4, 205, 0),
    ('Lentils', 0.70, 18, 230, 1), ('Yogurt', 1.10, 10, 150, 4),
    ('Peanut butter', 0.50, 8, 190, 16);

The query

DECIDE query
SELECT food, cost, servings
FROM Foods
DECIDE servings(INT)
SUCH THAT servings <= 3
      AND SUM(servings * protein) >= 90
      AND SUM(servings * calories) BETWEEN 1800 AND 2200
      AND SUM(servings * fat) <= 50
MINIMIZE SUM(servings * cost);

Problem class: ILP.

How it reads

  • servings <= 3
    Per row: no food more than three times.
  • SUM(servings * protein) >= 90
    A total over all foods.
  • BETWEEN 1800 AND 2200
    BETWEEN is two constraints, a floor and a ceiling, on the same sum.
  • MINIMIZE SUM(servings * cost)
    The cheapest plan that meets every target.

The answer

Three servings each of rice and lentils, two of peanut butter and one egg: 94 g of protein, 1,840 calories and 45 g of fat for 5.20. Chicken has the most protein per serving but costs too much per gram.
