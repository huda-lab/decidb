Raise revenue with as few price changes as possible

Reach a revenue target by changing prices, keeping the largest change small and touching at most two items.

MILP
decidb · price-changes.sql
SELECT item, price, ROUND(new_price, 2) AS new_price
FROM Menu
DECIDE new_price(REAL)
SUCH THAT new_price BETWEEN floor AND ceiling
      AND SUM(new_price * weekly_qty) >= 16500
      AND norm(new_price - price, 0) <= 2
MINIMIZE norm(new_price - price, 'inf');

The problem

A café needs weekly revenue of 16,500; current prices bring in 15,662.50. Each price may move within a floor and a ceiling. Customers notice big changes, and the menu board is a hassle to reprint, so: keep the biggest change as small as possible, and change at most two prices.

The data

SetupRuns as shown
CREATE TABLE Menu (item VARCHAR, price DOUBLE, weekly_qty INT, floor DOUBLE, ceiling DOUBLE);
INSERT INTO Menu VALUES
    ('Espresso', 3.00, 900, 2.50, 3.60), ('Latte', 4.50, 1200, 4.00, 5.40),
    ('Mocha', 5.00, 500, 4.50, 6.00), ('Tea', 2.50, 400, 2.00, 3.00),
    ('Muffin', 3.25, 700, 3.00, 3.90), ('Bagel', 2.75, 650, 2.50, 3.30);

The query

SUCH THAT new_price BETWEEN floor AND ceiling

Problem class: MILP, solved by Gurobi or HiGHS.

How it reads

new_price BETWEEN floor AND ceiling
Per-row bounds read from the data.
norm(new_price - price, 0) <= 2
The L0 norm counts the rows where the expression is nonzero: at most two prices change.
norm(new_price - price, 'inf')
The L∞ norm is the largest absolute change.

The answer

Espresso and latte each go up by 0.40, the busiest items, and nothing else changes. Without the L0 limit, the smallest largest change spreads across all six items, each up by about 0.19, so six prices change instead of two:
